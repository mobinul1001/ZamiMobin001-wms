It's one of the most confusing questions in business, because the quotes are all over the map. One agency promises the world for $5,000; another quotes $50,000 for what looks like the same thing. So let's cut through it with real numbers.

**Quick answer:** A website redesign costs between **$1,000 and $20,000+** in 2026, depending on scope. Most small business redesigns land in the **$1,000–$3,000** range, mid-size business sites run **$3,000–$6,000**, and large or enterprise redesigns start around $50,000 and climb well past $20,000. What you pay is driven far more by your site's size, functionality, and who builds it than by any fixed "market rate."

Below, we break down exactly what you get at each price level, the seven factors that move the number, the hidden costs that blindside people, and how to make sure you're spending the right amount for your business.

<a id="bysize"></a>

<p class="eyebrow">By Size</p>

## Website Redesign Cost by Site Size (2026)

The single biggest factor in what a redesign costs is how big and complex your site is. Here are realistic 2026 ranges based on agency pricing.

Small business site (5–15 pages)

$1,000–$3,000

A professional visual overhaul, mobile-friendly responsive design, on-page SEO setup, and CMS integration for a straightforward site with no complex backend. A note of caution: anything under about $700–$1,500 for a small business redesign usually means a barely-customized template, not a real redesign.

Mid-size business site (20–75 pages)

$3,000–$6,000

Custom design and development, lead-capture forms, blog, CRM or marketing integrations, QA testing across devices, and proper content migration. This is where most growing businesses land.

Large / enterprise site (100+ pages)

$8,000–$12,000+

Custom architecture, complex integrations, advanced UX flows, scalability requirements, and compliance needs. Large digital platforms can exceed $20,000.

Ecommerce redesign (any size)

$5,000–$20,000+

Online stores are their own category. Redesigning a WooCommerce, Shopify, or Magento store — with product taxonomies, payment-gateway integration, and checkout optimization — typically runs $5,000 to $50,000 or more, depending on the number of products and whether you need custom features like wishlists, subscriptions, or multi-currency support.

The pattern is simple: more pages, more custom design, and more functionality all push the price up, because each one adds design, development, and testing time.

<a id="factors"></a>

<p class="eyebrow">Cost Drivers</p>

## The 7 Factors That Determine Your Redesign Cost

Two businesses can both ask for "a redesign" and get quotes that differ by $30,000. These seven factors are why.

**Site size (number of pages).** The most influential factor by far. Every page needs design, development, and testing — even reused templates need layout adjustments and content alignment per page.

**Design approach.** A template-based refresh is a fraction of the cost of a fully custom build. Semi-custom (a purchased template tailored to your brand) sits in the middle. Fully custom design, unique illustrations, animations, and video cost the most.

**Functionality and features.** A brochure site is cheap. Add user accounts, payment gateways, booking systems, custom tools, API integrations, or multilingual support and the price climbs with each one.

**Content and copywriting.** Content is typically 10–25% of a redesign budget and is the most frequently under-estimated line item. If existing copy needs rewriting or new pages need writing, budget for it early — poor content undermines even the best design. Professional copywriting often runs $100–$300 per page.

**SEO and content migration.** Moving a content-heavy site without losing rankings takes real work: URL mapping, 301 redirects, metadata porting, structured data, and post-launch monitoring. Agencies often price this at $2,500–$20,000, either standalone or inside the redesign scope. Skipping it is how redesigns erase years of SEO overnight.

**Mobile optimization.** With most traffic now on phones and Google using mobile-first indexing, mobile optimization is more than resizing a desktop layout — it means touch-friendly navigation, compressed images, and strong Core Web Vitals scores. More device testing means more cost, but stronger rankings and conversions.

**Who builds it.** This is a huge swing. Freelancers cost less than agencies; agencies cost more but bring a team, a process, and accountability for results. In-house can look cheapest on paper but often hides costs in staff time and delays. DIY AI website builders are cheapest of all, but tend to produce generic, non-branded results that still need development to deploy well.

<a id="vs"></a>

<p class="eyebrow">Right-Size It</p>

## Redesign vs Refresh: Which Do You Actually Need?

Here's where a lot of money gets wasted — paying for a full redesign when a refresh would have solved the problem. There are really four levels:

-   **Redecoration (cheapest):** cosmetic updates only, like new brand colors. Low cost because it needs little design work.
-   **Refresh:** update the homepage layout, typography, colors, and key templates while keeping the existing platform. Fast and affordable.
-   **Redesign:** rebuild the structure, templates, and UX, usually with a new look on top. The standard "redesign" most people mean.
-   **Rebuild (most expensive):** a brand-new site, often on a new platform, reusing only some assets. Warranted for major rebrands or outdated platforms.

If your site works but looks tired, you may only need a refresh — and save thousands. If it's failing on performance, platform, or multiple fronts, a full redesign is justified. Diagnosing this correctly *before* you request quotes is the easiest way to avoid overpaying.

<a id="hidden"></a>

<p class="eyebrow">Watch Out</p>

## Hidden Costs That Catch Businesses Off Guard

The headline redesign price is rarely the whole story. Budget for these so a $12,000 project doesn't quietly become $16,000:

-   **Custom photography:** $3,000–$3,000 if you need original imagery.
-   **Video production:** $5,000–$25,000 per video.
-   **Premium plugins, tools, and subscriptions:** recurring annual costs for the software that powers the new site.
-   **Domain and hosting:** ongoing, often overlooked at quote time.
-   **Content creation:** the most common budget-buster when copy isn't ready.
-   **Ongoing maintenance:** typically 15–20% of the build cost per year to keep the new site secure, updated, and fast.

That last one matters most for your long-term budget. A redesign is not a one-time purchase — without ongoing maintenance, the new site decays and you're paying for another redesign far sooner than you should.

<a id="roi"></a>

<p class="eyebrow">ROI</p>

## Is a Website Redesign Worth It? How to Think About ROI

The cost only makes sense next to the return. The right way to frame a redesign is as an investment with measurable outcomes, not an expense.

Do the math for your own site. If a redesign costs $12,000 and lifts your conversion rate by even a couple of percentage points, what's that worth against your annual traffic? Businesses commonly justify redesigns through mobile-conversion gains, lower bounce rates, faster load times, better SEO, and improved brand trust — and many recover the cost within 6–12 months. One useful test: if your average customer is worth $1,000 and the redesign costs $12,000, you break even on just six new customers.

The flip side is the cost of **inaction**. An outdated, slow, or non-mobile site loses trust, leaks SEO, and sends visitors to competitors every day — a quieter but very real cost.

<a id="budget"></a>

<p class="eyebrow">Smart Spending</p>

## How to Budget for Your Redesign (and Not Overpay)

A few practical rules to land on the right number:

Match the spend to the goal. A local business that needs a clean, modern, mobile-friendly site does not need a $8,000 enterprise build. Define the specific problems the redesign must solve before you talk to anyone.

Get itemized quotes. Ask exactly what's included — design, development, content, SEO migration, testing, and post-launch support — so you're comparing deliverables, not just bottom-line numbers.

Budget for content and maintenance from the start. These are the two lines that turn a "cheap" redesign expensive later.

Don't over-buy. Paying for custom functionality you'll never use is as wasteful as under-investing in a site that can't grow with you.
