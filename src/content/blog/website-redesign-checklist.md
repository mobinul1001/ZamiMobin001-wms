This is that checklist. It walks through every phase of a redesign in order — planning, SEO preparation, design, development, pre-launch QA, launch, and post-launch — so nothing critical slips through. Work through it top to bottom, and bookmark it to check off as you go.

<a id="phase1"></a>

<p class="pair"><strong>1</strong> <span>Planning and Discovery Checklist</span></p>

Skipping this phase is why so many redesigns look nice but fail to perform. Nail down the strategy before anyone touches a design.

-   **Define the goal.** Name the specific problems the redesign must solve — low conversions, poor mobile experience, dated look, slow speed, or an outdated platform. "A fresh look" is not a goal.
-   **Set measurable targets.** Record your current conversion rate, traffic, bounce rate, and load times so you can prove the redesign worked later.
-   **Understand your audience.** Know who your visitors are and what they need from the site.
-   **Study competitors.** Look at what's working on competing sites to inform (not copy) your direction.
-   **Audit your current content.** Catalog every page and decide what to keep, rewrite, merge, or remove.
-   **Choose your platform.** Confirm where the new site will live and why (WordPress remains the flexible default for most businesses).
-   **Set a realistic budget and timeline.** Most small-to-mid business redesigns run 8–12 weeks; budget for content and post-launch maintenance, not just the build.
-   **Assign one decision-maker.** Designate a single point of contact with final approval authority to prevent stakeholder gridlock — the biggest cause of delays.
-   **Prepare your content early.** Gather copy, images, logo, and brand assets before design starts. This alone can cut the timeline by 25–40%.

<a id="phase2"></a>

<p class="pair"><strong>2</strong> <span>The Pre-Redesign SEO Checklist</span></p>

This is the phase that protects your rankings, and the one most often skipped. Do it before development begins, because you can't protect what you haven't measured.

-   **Crawl your entire current site** to capture every live URL, title tag, and meta description (tools like Screaming Frog work well).
-   **Identify your top-performing pages** by traffic, rankings, and conversions in Google Search Console and analytics — these are protected at all costs.
-   **Find your most-linked pages.** Note which pages have the most backlinks, since they carry the most ranking authority.
-   **Export your current rankings and traffic** as a "before" benchmark to compare against after launch.
-   **Back up everything** — your site files, database, and old XML sitemap.
-   **Do keyword research** for any new pages or restructured content.
-   **Check current page speed and Core Web Vitals** so you can compare after launch.

<a id="phase3"></a>

<p class="pair"><strong>3</strong> <span>Design and UX Checklist</span></p>

With strategy set, move to how the site looks and works.

-   **Map the new site structure and navigation** before designing any page.
-   **Create wireframes** for each page type to lock in layout and user flow before visual design.
-   **Design mobile-first.** Most traffic is on phones, so design for thumbs and small screens first.
-   **Design the homepage first**, then interior page templates — the homepage sets the tone for everything.
-   **Keep navigation simple and intuitive.** Cut links that rarely get clicked; make key pages easy to reach.
-   **Design clear calls to action** on every important page so visitors know the next step.
-   **Ensure brand consistency** across colors, typography, and imagery.
-   **Plan for accessibility** (WCAG 2.2 AA is the current standard expectation).
-   **Consolidate feedback** into one clear round per design to avoid endless revisions.

<a id="phase4"></a>

<p class="pair"><strong>4</strong> <span>Development and Content Checklist</span></p>

Build the site on staging, never live.

-   **Build on a staging environment** blocked from Google indexing (noindex or password protection).
-   **Build the complete 301 redirect map** — every old URL matched one-to-one to its new equivalent. This is the single most important technical task.
-   **Keep existing URLs wherever possible;** only change them for a real reason, and avoid redirect chains.
-   **Migrate content carefully**, preserving the text on pages that already rank.
-   **Carry over on-page SEO** — title tags, meta descriptions, H1s, and image alt text — and refine rather than rewrite from scratch.
-   **Reimplement schema markup** (FAQ, LocalBusiness, Product) that existed on the old site.
-   **Rebuild internal links** to point directly at the new URLs.
-   **Optimize images and code** for fast loading.
-   **Set up analytics and conversion tracking** (Google Analytics, tag manager, goals) on the new build.
-   **Test all forms, integrations, and interactive elements** on staging.

<a id="phase5"></a>

<p class="pair"><strong>5</strong> <span>Pre-Launch Checklist</span></p>

This is your last chance to catch problems before real users and real rankings are affected.

-   **Crawl the staging site** and compare URL count, titles, and meta descriptions against your Phase 2 audit — resolve any discrepancies.
-   **Test a sample of your 301 redirects** to confirm they resolve directly and correctly.
-   **Run Core Web Vitals and page-speed tests** and fix any regressions.
-   **Test on real mobile devices,** not just a resized browser.
-   **Crawl for broken links and 404 errors** within the new structure.
-   **Proofread every page** for typos and broken formatting.
-   **Confirm analytics and conversion tracking fire correctly.**
-   **Check all forms** submit and route properly.
-   **Verify the staging noindex is ready to be removed** at launch (forgetting this hides your live site from Google).
-   **Confirm your XML sitemap and robots.txt** are correct.
-   **Take a final backup** of the old site before switching.

<a id="phase6"></a>

<p class="pair"><strong>6</strong> <span>Launch Day Checklist</span></p>

-   **Remove the staging noindex** so Google can crawl the live site. (Double-check this — it's a classic, costly mistake.)
-   **Switch the domain** to the new site.
-   **Confirm 301 redirects are firing** server-side on the live site.
-   **Submit your new XML sitemap** to Google Search Console immediately.
-   **Verify both old and new site** in Search Console if the domain changed.
-   **Test the live site** — homepage, key pages, forms, and a sample of redirects.
-   **Monitor closely** for the first 48–72 hours for any issues.
-   **Time your launch well** — aim for February–May or September–October, and avoid launching right before the holidays.

<a id="phase7"></a>

<p class="pair"><strong>7</strong> <span>Post-Launch Checklist</span></p>

The work isn't done at launch. The first month determines whether your rankings survive.

-   **Monitor Google Search Console daily for 30 days** for 404 errors and crawl issues, fixing them immediately.
-   **Compare rankings and traffic** against your pre-launch benchmark.
-   **Expect a small dip** in the first 1–2 weeks (10–20% is normal), with recovery by week 4–6. If you're still 30%+ below baseline at week 8, something is wrong — usually redirects, metadata, or internal links.
-   **Fix broken links and redirect gaps** as they appear.
-   **Keep 301 redirects live for at least a year.**
-   **Gather user feedback** on the new site and refine.
-   **Set up ongoing maintenance** so the new site stays secure, fast, and current — this is what delays your next redesign for years.

<a id="industry"></a>

<p class="eyebrow">Industry Note</p>

## Your Checklist May Need Extra Steps

The phases above apply to every redesign, but some industries have added requirements worth flagging: ecommerce sites need product-catalog migration, payment-gateway testing, and checkout optimization; healthcare and legal sites need compliance and privacy checks; nonprofits often need donation-flow and integration testing; and contractors, real estate, and local businesses should double down on local SEO and mobile, since most of their traffic is on-the-go and location-based. Whatever your industry, the seven phases stay the same — you're just adding the checks specific to your site's functionality.
